problem: >

  bring a brand new product type into an app built for speakers, and define a
  light control experience that felt effortless at a party yet powerful enough to
  personalise, all while feeling unmistakably SOUNDBOKS. Get it wrong and a light
  meant to set the mood becomes one more thing to fiddle with in the dark.

insightHeading: What testing surfaced
insight: >
  The team ran research at every stage: competitive analysis, personas and two
  rounds of app usability testing, then external testing with recruited SOUNDBOKS
  owners and tests at real parties. The signal was consistent. Audio reactivity
  was the hero; when people saw the light move to the music it felt like a real
  party and they stopped fiddling. Custom colours and memorable palette names
  delighted. Battery life was a real anxiety, with people dimming the light
  unnecessarily because they could not tell how much charge was left. And testers
  valued that Lightboks looked and behaved like the SOUNDBOKS they already trusted.

decisionHeading: The key decision
decision: >
  Design for effortless first, control second. Lead with audio reactivity so the
  light just works out of the box, then layer user owned, predictable
  customisation on top: colours, energy levels and effects. And make the colour
  palettes abstract and universal rather than themed, so they read the same for
  everyone instead of leaning on culturally specific ideas.

## How we ran it

The work ran as a six phase process: Understand, Diverge, Reflect, Wireframe and
internal testing, Prototype, and Test, across the product manager, the light team
and engineering. That meant framing the product with stakeholder interviews and a
value proposition canvas, prioritising the MVP against SOUNDBOKS's USPs,
facilitating Crazy 8s and critique workshops, and running the colour, energy level
and effects systems from first sketches to a high fidelity Figma prototype.

Alongside the flows the team owned the content and colour strategy: defining the
generative colour palettes with their values and behaviour, and the naming system,
and making the call to move away from themed palettes toward abstract, universal
ones. Every round of research, from usability sessions to external testing with
recruited owners and tests at real parties, fed the next iteration, right up to the
launched product.
