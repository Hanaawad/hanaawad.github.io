insightHeading: What testing surfaced
insight: >
  I ran research at every stage — competitive analysis, personas and two rounds of
  app usability testing, then external testing with recruited SOUNDBOKS owners and
  in-the-wild party tests. The signal was consistent: audio-reactivity was the hero
  — when people saw the light move to the music, it "felt like a real party" and
  they stopped fiddling. Custom colours and memorable palette names delighted;
  battery life was a real anxiety (people dimmed the light unnecessarily because
  they couldn't tell how much charge was left); and testers valued that Lightboks
  looked and behaved like the SOUNDBOKS they already trusted.

decisionHeading: The key decision
decision: >
  Design for effortless first, control second. Lead with audio-reactivity so the
  light just works out of the box, then layer user-owned, predictable customisation
  — colours, energy levels and effects — on top. And make the colour palettes
  abstract and universal rather than themed, so they read the same for everyone
  instead of leaning on culturally-specific ideas.

## How I led it

I structured the work as a six-phase process — Understand, Diverge, Reflect,
Wireframe & internal testing, Prototype, and Test — and ran it across the product
manager, the light team and engineering. That meant framing the product with
stakeholder interviews and a value-proposition canvas, prioritising the MVP against
SOUNDBOKS's USPs, facilitating Crazy 8s and critique workshops, and running the
colour, energy-level and effects systems from first sketches to a high-fidelity
Figma prototype.

Alongside the flows I owned the content and colour strategy: defining the
generative colour palettes (with their hex values and behaviour) and the naming
system, and making the call to move away from themed palettes toward abstract,
universal ones. Every round of research — usability sessions, external testing with
recruited owners, and real-world party tests — fed the next iteration, right up to
the launched product.
