## Design direction

The brief was premium but quiet. An accessory has to look considered without shouting,
and the content has to sit calmly inside a big retail catalogue. Every choice below
serves that, and one more thing: being reused across products that have nothing in
common but the brand.

### Layout

The whole system is built from **modules on a grid**, a hero, a feature section with
annotated callouts, spec blocks, the fit section, and a lifestyle close. Each one is
self-contained, so a new product swaps the photo and the numbers and keeps the layout.
Generous whitespace does the premium work, and the product shots sit on the page with a
`multiply` blend so they ground into the paper instead of floating in a box.

### Colour

A warm off-white (**#F9F6F0**) ground with near-black ink (**#1C1915**) and a single
muted steel-sage accent (**#5D6057**). The restraint is deliberate: a neutral base is
what lets one template carry a black watch, tan leather, green canvas and polished steel
without a fight, and it lets each product's own material drive the tone. The one dark
band at the end of every A+ page, for the lifestyle shot, is the only place the value
flips, so it always lands as a moment.

### Type

**Montserrat** throughout, a clean geometric sans with a Scandinavian, catalogue feel.
Wide letter-spacing on the small labels gives it a considered, editorial rhythm; the
headlines stay light-weight and roomy so nothing reads like a hard sell.

### The fit scale

The keystone of the system. Instead of a vague "fits most", the fit section draws a real
measured scale in the category's own unit, **14.5 to 20.5 cm** on the wrist for the Tide,
**up to 17.3 inches** for the laptop bag, **15 to 16 cm** for a size-M bracelet. It is
generated from the measurements, so it is accurate, and it is one component, so it is
identical across desktop, mobile, and every product in the catalogue. Honest information,
drawn once, reused everywhere, which is the whole idea of the project in a single element.

### How it scales

Because the page is a kit of modules and parametric components rather than a bespoke
layout, a new product, or a new size or colour variant of an existing one, is produced by
swapping photography and numbers, not by redrawing. That is what makes it work as a
catalogue system: consistent and premium at the front, fast and repeatable behind.

In practice that meant a lot of variants. The three products shown here, the Tide watch,
the Montreal XL bag and the Icon bracelet, are each one example of many: I produced this
content across the line's sizes, colourways and materials, every variant getting the same
full seven-image PDP set, A+ page and fit scale, all generated from the one system rather
than drawn from scratch.
