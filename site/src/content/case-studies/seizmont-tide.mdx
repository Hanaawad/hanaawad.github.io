## Design direction

The brief was premium but quiet. A dive watch has to look capable without shouting,
and the content had to sit calmly inside a big retail catalogue. Every choice below
serves that, and serves one more thing: being reused.

### Layout

The whole system is built from **modules on a grid**: a hero, a feature section with
annotated callouts, spec blocks, the fit section, and a lifestyle close. Each one is
self-contained, so a new variation swaps the photo and the numbers and keeps the
layout. Generous whitespace does the premium work, and the product shots sit on the
page with a `multiply` blend so they ground into the paper instead of floating in a
box.

### Colour

A warm off-white (**#F9F6F0**) ground with near-black ink (**#1C1915**) and a single
muted steel-sage accent (**#5D6057**). The restraint is deliberate: a black watch
needs a quiet, warm background to read clearly, and a neutral palette is what lets the
same template carry every strap colour in the line without a fight. The one dark band
at the end, for the lifestyle shot, is the only place the value flips, so it lands as a
moment.

### Type

**Montserrat** throughout, a clean geometric sans with a Scandinavian, catalogue feel.
Wide letter-spacing on the small labels (`SEIZMONT / TIDE`, `MADE TO BE READ`) gives it
a considered, editorial rhythm; the headlines stay light-weight and roomy so nothing
feels like a hard sell.

### The size scale

The piece I'm most happy with. Instead of a vague "fits most wrists", the fit section
draws a real measured scale from **14.5 to 20.5 cm** with the case, lug-to-lug,
thickness and strap numbers beside it. It's generated from the measurements, so it's
accurate, and it's a component, so it's identical on desktop, on mobile, and on every
other variation in the line. Honest information, drawn once, reused everywhere, which
is the whole idea of the project in one element.
