overview: >
  Ovni connects travellers with buyers. Working alongside the design and
  development team, I led the UX and also wrote front-end code — taking the
  concept from a brand-new idea in Egypt toward an easy-to-use proof of concept.

problem: >
  Buyers often want products that are only available in other countries, but it
  isn't always possible for them to travel and get what they want. Ovni develops
  an easy-to-use platform and app that connects travellers with buyers: a buyer
  makes a wish and adds all the details of the product they'd like, so a traveller
  heading to that destination can contact them and bring the product back for a
  delivery fee.

goals:
  - title: Prove the concept
    body: Develop an MVP to serve as a proof of concept.
  - title: Grow both sides
    body: Acquire as many buyers and travellers as possible.
  - title: An easy, brand-new interface
    body: Develop an easy-to-use interface, given that the concept is brand new in Egypt.
  - title: Web-first, app on its way
    body: Design a friendly website while the app is on its way to the App Store.
  - title: Mobile & tablet
    body: Introduce mobile and tablet optimisation.
  - title: Learn for the app
    body: After the proof of concept, gather information from travellers and buyers to highlight pain points to avoid while designing the mobile app.

methods:
  - Customer interviews
  - Market research analysis
  - Prototype testing
  - Persona

  - Information architecture

insightHeading: Gathering insights
insight: >
  After collecting the recordings from the user interviews, I conducted affinity
  mapping with my teammate to synthesise the pains identified. We grouped these
  problems under common themes and features across the platform — and created
  personas to recognise that different people have different needs and expectations.
insightImage: ovni-affinity

decisionHeading: The key decision
decision: >
  Ship a web-first proof of concept and learn from real travellers and buyers
  before building the mobile app — so the app could be designed around evidenced
  pain points rather than assumptions.
