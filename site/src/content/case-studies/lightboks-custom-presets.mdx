overview: >
  SOUNDBOKS owners increasingly wanted to make their setup their own — saving the EQ that
  sounds right for a room, or dialling in the exact colour for their Lightboks lighting.
  As Senior UX Design Lead I drove a system of user-owned custom presets: designing and
  testing custom colours for Lightboks, and building the business case for custom EQ — with
  presets that belong to the person and follow them across every SOUNDBOKS product.

problem: >
  People were recreating the same settings over and over. From research: "I find myself
  remembering the settings for each placement — it could be way easier to save the settings
  with a name and then simply choose between them," and "I wish I had more EQ presets for
  different room settings & layouts." The same was true for light — owners wanted to set,
  save and reuse their own colours, not just pick from a fixed list.

goals:
  - title: Let people make it their own
    body: Move beyond fixed defaults — let users create and save their own presets, for both colour and EQ.
  - title: Presets that belong to the user
    body: Store presets as user-specific settings that apply to any product, rather than living on a single device.
  - title: One system of custom settings
    body: Lay the foundation for a broader system of custom settings — EQ, colours, animations and Pro Panel.
  - title: A measurable outcome
    body: Hit the OKR — 10%+ of users create at least one custom preset, with positive feedback and improved ratings.

methods:
  - Customer interviews
  - User testing (6 participants)
  - Business case & OKRs
  - User flows
  - Hi-fi design (Figma)
  - Prototyping
processImage: process
processCaption: Iterating the colour-preset flows and validating them across rounds of user testing.

insightHeading: What testing surfaced
insight: >
  Testing the colour experience with six participants sharpened the design. "Default" and
  "presets" were competing terms, so the default colours became a clear shuffle action. We
  probed whether the colour editor, the number of colour choices, the colour wheel and hex
  #code entry were understood — and whether people preferred tapping to change a colour or an
  automatic mode. Each round fed straight back into the flows.
insightImage: flows

decisionHeading: The key decision
decision: >
  Make custom presets belong to the user, not the device. Rather than storing names on each
  product, presets are user-specific settings applied to any product — using a transformation
  matrix so the same preset translates across product models. It's the foundation for one
  system of custom settings across the whole SOUNDBOKS ecosystem.

features:
  - title: Colour creator
    icon: profiles
    body: Build a custom colour from presets, a colour wheel, a hex #code or a monochrome flow — then save it as your own.
  - title: AutoColor & presets
    icon: dot
    body: A library of named colour presets and an AutoColor mode to set the mood in a tap.
  - title: Custom EQ presets
    icon: eq
    body: Save and name multiple EQs — one for a room, one for a genre — and switch between them instead of re-dialling every time.
  - title: Presets that follow you
    icon: users
    body: Presets are tied to the person, not the speaker, so they apply across any SOUNDBOKS product you connect to.
  - title: Edit & manage
    icon: panel
    body: Edit, shuffle and delete presets — with a clear warning when a preset in use is removed from multiple products.

outcome: >
  The colour-preset experience was designed and validated through user testing, and the
  Custom EQ business case set the strategy — success metrics, cross-functional dependencies
  (backend storage & sync, product design, and firmware/DSP) and the MVP plan — to bring
  user-owned presets to the whole SOUNDBOKS lineup.
